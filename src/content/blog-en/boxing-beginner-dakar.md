## Boxing is not reserved for athletes

Many people think you must "already be fit" to step into a ring. Wrong. Boxing is one of the most complete sports to start with: cardio, strength, coordination, mindset. Everything is trained at the same time, at your own pace.

At Lifitness, most of our beginner boxers had never hit a bag before their first session. Three months later, they chain rounds without stopping.

## What boxing will give you

- **Real cardio.** Boxing sessions raise your heart rate for a sustained period. Result: more endurance in daily life, less shortness of breath.
- **A full-body workout.** Every punch starts in the legs, travels through the core and ends in the shoulder. You work everything without even noticing.
- **A clearer head.** Hitting a bag after a workday is a formidable outlet. Stress drops, concentration rises.
- **Confidence.** Knowing how to hold a guard, move and take the effort changes your posture — literally and figuratively.

## Your first week: the simple plan

1. **Session 1 — Learn the guard and the jab.** No power, no speed. Just the position: feet shoulder-width apart, hands high, chin tucked.
2. **Session 2 — Add footwork.** Step forward, backward, turn without crossing your feet. Boxing is won with the feet before the fists.
3. **Session 3 — Combine.** Jab, cross, hook. Slowly. Speed will come once the movement is clean.

Between sessions, sleep and hydrate. Dakar heats up fast: drink before you feel thirsty.

## The gear to get started

The minimum is very little:

- Comfortable sportswear.
- Wraps to protect your wrists.
- Boxing gloves (12 to 16 oz depending on your build).

For your first trial session, no need to invest: we lend you the equipment. Buy your own gloves only once the routine is set, after two or three weeks.

## The 4 classic beginner mistakes

1. **Hitting too hard, too soon.** Power is a consequence of technique, never the other way around.
2. **Dropping your hands.** The guard tires the shoulders at first. That is normal. Keep it up: it is your life insurance in the ring.
3. **Holding your breath.** Exhale with every punch. One round holding your breath and the session is over.
4. **Wanting to spar right away.** Sparring comes later, once technique is stable and supervised.

## How to progress fast (legally)

- **Two sessions a week minimum.** Boxing is a language: you have to speak it often.
- **Film yourself.** Three rounds on the bag filmed on a phone beat every feeling in the world to correct a movement.
- **Get corrected.** A coach who repositions your elbow saves you a month of trial and error alone.
