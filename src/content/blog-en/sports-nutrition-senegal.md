## No need for imported products to eat well

Supermarket "sports" aisles sell overpriced powders. Meanwhile, the Senegalese market is full of everything an athlete needs: fresh fish, peanut, millet, fonio, black-eyed peas, fruits, vegetables. Your plate is already here.

This article does not replace a nutritionist. It gives concrete guidelines to build your meals around training, with what you find in Dakar.

## The foundation: three priorities

1. **Protein at every meal.** Fish (thiof, capitaine, sardinella), chicken, lean beef, eggs, black-eyed peas, peanut. This is the building block of muscle. Without it, training builds nothing.
2. **Carbs matched to the effort.** Rice, fonio, millet, sweet potato, cassava. Fonio and millet have a lower glycemic index than white rice: favor them on rest days.
3. **Serious hydration.** In Dakar, you sweat constantly. Aim for 2.5 to 3 liters of water a day, more during the session.

## Around the session: what to eat, when?

**2 to 3 hours before training:** a complete but digestible meal. Examples:

- Thieboudienne in a reasonable portion (the fish brings protein, the rice energy).
- Chicken yassa with rice.
- Fonio + leaf sauce + fish.

**Less than an hour before:** keep it light. A banana, a few dates, a bouye juice with no added sugar.

**Within the hour after:** protein + carbs to repair and refuel. A chicken-egg sandwich, curdled milk with millet, black-eyed peas with rice, or a protein shake if you already use one.

**In the evening after a late session (9-11pm):** do not skip the meal. A smaller portion is enough: omelette, grilled fish, vegetables. Muscle repairs at night, not during the session.

## Local allies to favor

- **Fish.** Dakar is one of the best places in the world to eat it. Fresh, varied, rich in protein and omega-3. Enjoy it freely.
- **Peanut.** Energetic, protein-rich, affordable. As a paste in mafé or roasted as a snack. Watch the quantities: it is calorie-dense.
- **Black-eyed peas (niebe).** The most accessible plant protein at the market. Excellent in salad or in sauce.
- **Bouye (baobab fruit).** Rich in calcium and vitamin C. As a juice with no added sugar, it is one of the best local drinks.
- **Bissap.** Antioxidant, refreshing. As long as you drink it lightly sweetened — a glass of street bissap can contain the equivalent of several sugar cubes.
- **Mango, papaya, guava.** Local, seasonal fruits, cheaper and more nutrient-dense than imported fruit.

## The classic traps in Dakar

1. **Sugary juices.** Bissap, ginger, bouye: excellent products, often drowned in sugar. Ask for lightly sweetened, or make them yourself.
2. **Excess oil.** Mafé and some sauces are generous with peanut oil. Delicious, but calorific: one normal portion, not two.
3. **Mountains of rice.** Restaurant rice portions often exceed double your real needs. Eat the fish and vegetables first, the rice as a side.
4. **Eating "light" after a hard session.** This is mistake number 1 for those who want to build muscle. After the effort, the body asks for nutrients. Give them to it.
5. **Café Touba late in the day.** Excellent, but high in sugar and stimulating: after 5pm, it can sabotage your sleep — and therefore your recovery.

## An example day (training at 7pm)

- **Breakfast:** 3-egg omelette, whole-grain bread, lightly sweetened coffee, a mango.
- **Lunch:** thieboudienne, normal portion, glass of water.
- **Snack at 5pm:** handful of roasted peanuts + banana.
- **Session 7pm-8:30pm**, continuous hydration.
- **Dinner at 9:15pm:** chicken yassa, moderate portion of rice, vegetables.

## The final word

The best athlete diet is not the most exotic or the most expensive. It is the most consistent. Protein at every meal, local and fresh foods, plenty of water, sleep. Hold that for three months of serious training and you will see the difference in the mirror.
